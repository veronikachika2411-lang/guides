01 · The methodThe main part

Three rules that made it

01 · Tension, not wordsNobody needs to be told "don't scroll". A dart falling at a balloon does the job by itself
02 · One cameraEvery shot flows into the next. A camera that jumps between shots feels like lag
03 · Show the insideThe twist is the build: the same scene taken apart and put back together by the prompt

Why it holds attention

The viewer is waiting for one thing: the pop. As long as the dart has not hit, they stay. The ruler counting down in millimetres and the focus square make the wait feel physical, like someone is really filming it.

Real zoom, not a dolly

The camera does not fly closer. It stands still and narrows the lens, like a phone zoom: the background flattens, the shake grows, focus hunts for a moment. Three jerks with a small overshoot sell it.

The tangent trick

To see a 0.4 mm gap, the camera has to look exactly along the surface of the balloon. Then the point of impact sits on the edge of the silhouette and the gap shows against the light background.

04 · The briefCopy all of it

The reel brief

Replace what is in square brackets with your own words, colours and handle. The rest can stay.

Prompt · showreel brief
Build a 3D showreel in three.js inside Remotion. 34 seconds, 30 fps, 1080x1920, H.264 CRF 18, yuv420p. All on-screen text in English. Keep text away from the Instagram buttons (top 220 px, bottom 380 px).

IDEA
A dart falls toward a [blue] balloon and stops 0.4 mm before it. The viewer waits for the pop. Then I tell them none of it is real, show how it was built, and pop it on "move it".

THE SCENE
A glossy rubber balloon (lathe geometry, clearcoat), a steel dart with a knurled barrel and black flights, a light cream studio with soft shadow on the floor. Both made from code, no downloaded models.

ONE CAMERA
The camera is one continuous move. Shots must blend into each other over 20-30 frames. No jumps between shots: I see them as lag. Handheld shake measured as a share of the frame, so it looks the same on a wide lens and on a telephoto.

PARTS
1. THE FALL (0-1.2 s). The dart falls from high above, spinning on its axis. The camera falls right behind the flights, looking down at the balloon growing below. No text, no voice: the picture must hook on its own.
2. THE APPROACH (1.2-7.6 s). The dart brakes like a slow-motion camera kicked in. The operator stops and zooms in with three jerky optical zooms, focus hunting after each one. A red focus square like a phone camera locks on the tip, jumps to the rubber, comes back to the gap. A ruler next to the gap shows the distance in mm, counting down to 0.4.
3. THE FREEZE (7.6-12 s). Time stops. The camera orbits around the frozen gap. Voice: "You just watched a dart not hit a balloon for seven seconds."
4. NOT REAL (12-15 s). Pull back. A scan line turns everything into wireframe from top to bottom, then the wire balloon folds back into a single profile line and the dart flies apart.
5. THE BUILD (15-22 s). A [Claude Code] window with my prompt. Step by step it rebuilds the scene: the profile spins into a balloon, grey clay turns into glossy rubber, the studio lights switch on, the dart assembles part by part, the shaky hand turns on. End with "[N] lines. 0 editing apps."
6. YOUR BRAND (22-26 s). The balloon turns and brings "YOUR BRAND" printed on it into view. "Now imagine it was your brand. People can't look away."
7. THE POP (26-31 s). "So stop waiting." Two jerky zooms back into the gap. On "Move it" the balloon pops: the tear opens in petals from the tip, the rubber wrinkles and curls back, a puff of powder, a short shockwave, a dozen big shreds. The dart rushes in, almost stops inside the bursting balloon while the camera orbits, then rushes out.
8. END CARD. "[I make brands move.]" "made with code + Claude" "Comment [DART]".

TEXT
No blur and no shake on text. Each line appears with opacity only.

SOUND
Synthesize the music in numpy, no samples: wind on the fall, a heartbeat that speeds up, zoom clicks, silence on the freeze, a bell on every build step, a riser into the pop, then a drop with an 808 and a cowbell. Mix the voice files on top, duck the music under the voice. Master to -14 LUFS for stereo.

WORKFLOW
1. Create the files. Type-check must pass.
2. Render about 24 key frames into one contact sheet and look at it yourself.
3. Self-QA: text over the dart, text on a busy background, objects out of frame. Fix and re-render.
4. Render the final mp4 with sound.
5. Measure the frame-to-frame difference of the finished file with ffmpeg (tblend + signalstats) and list every spike. Every spike must be a planned zoom jerk or the pop. Anything else is a camera jump: fix it.
6. Tell me in one message: file path, what you checked and what you could not check.

Do not stop until there is a finished mp4.
05 · NotesIn plain words

How to fix it with notes

The first render is never the final one. Watch it and say what is wrong. These are close to the notes I actually sent:

The colours are off
I don't like the palette and the dark background. Keep the balloon blue, make the studio light and clean.
I want to see the distance
In the close-up, write the distance between the tip and the balloon right next to the gap.
Show the inside
Show how it all gets built from the inside, like a screen recording of the process.
It lags
Not enough movement, somewhere it feels laggy and somewhere it is not logical. Make the zoom more real and the pop stronger.
Kill the words at the start
Remove the words at the start. The viewer should want to watch on their own: the dart should rush at the balloon from the first frame.
The opening
Make the dart fall from above and the camera fall fast right behind it. When it gets close to the balloon, the camera settles into the side view.
06 · HonestWhat to know

What broke for me

  • The gap was invisible. With the camera a little above the surface, the tip landed on the rubber in the picture and the gap disappeared. Looking exactly along the surface fixed it.
  • The camera jumped between shots. Each shot had its own camera and the position snapped at every join. One continuous camera with blended shots fixed the "lag".
  • A one-frame cut in the middle. The wireframe scan crossed a tiny telephoto frame in a single frame and looked like a glitch. Starting the scan after the pull-back fixed it. The frame-difference check from the brief is how I found it.
  • A shadow without a balloon. After the pop the floor still showed the full balloon shadow, because shadows ignore the torn surface. Switch the balloon's shadow off when it pops.
  • Claude cannot hear or see motion. It checks still frames and numbers. How the reel feels is yours to judge, so expect several rounds.
  • Slow render. Two 3D layers at 1080×1920 can take a while. It is normal.
07 · QuestionsShort

Common questions

Will I get the same reel?

Similar in structure, not frame for frame: Claude writes the code from scratch each time. Change the colours, the object and the words to make it yours.

The terminal shows a red error. Now what?

Copy it and paste it to Claude with the words “here is the error, fix it and continue”.

Can it be a bullet and a can instead?

Yes. Swap the objects in the brief. Keep the rules: one continuous camera, a measured gap, and the build in the middle.
