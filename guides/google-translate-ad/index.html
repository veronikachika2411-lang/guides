What you get: the method behind a premium vertical ad, the structure that makes it feel expensive, and one prompt to start with. Swap the brand and the same recipe works for another app.

A 17.5 second ad that asks one question in the first second and answers it in the last frame.

Google Translate is a translation app by Google. This is an unofficial concept ad by Veronika, not affiliated with Google and not commissioned by it.

Claude Code / Remotion / 1080 x 1920 / about 17.5 seconds / 120 BPM
01 / the idea

Study the brand first

Look at the real campaigns before you write a scene. Google Translate stories are about people, not features. So the ad is built around a feeling.

One question: “Can we stay close in different languages?” The last frame answers it: “Every language. Still close.”
1The app icon appears out of a glow.
2The hook question, then the word “close” spins through languages.
3A real number: almost 250 languages, on a mechanical counter.
4Four features, each with its own kind of picture: a message from Nonna, a finger drawing a character, a face to face split screen, an offline download ring.
5A second real number: 1 trillion words translated every month, as a wall of words.
6The button opens back into the icon. Slogan.

Use only real facts and put the source on screen.

02 / premium picture

What makes it look expensive

  • One world. A real piece of the app interface stays on screen and changes with each scene: the bottom bar with two languages and the translate button. In the split screen it becomes the dividing line.
  • One hero. The translate button. The icon shrinks into it at the start and opens back into the icon at the end.
  • A color per scene. Blue, red, yellow, green, and all four at the end.
  • Frosted glass, not flat cards. Put slowly moving colored blobs behind the glass, otherwise there is nothing to blur.
  • Light. A shine band runs across the button on every tap.
  • Big interface. Fill the bottom of the frame. No film grain.
Leave room for Instagram. Keep text and buttons away from the bottom and the right edge, where the app puts its own buttons.
03 / energy and sound

Make it move, make it sound

  • Speed ramps. Transitions run 1.5 to 2 times faster, reading moments stay calm. One time-warp table drives both picture and sound, so they never drift apart.
  • Cuts on the beat. At 120 BPM and 30 fps that is a cut every 15 frames.
  • The camera always moves: a slow push-in, a quick pull-back on the cut, a small kick on every beat.
  • Headlines land with weight. They rise from a mask and settle. No blur, no shake.
  • Three tricks from the brand itself: the hook word spinning through languages, a mechanical number counter, and a number that turns into a picture.
  • Sound on the exact frame. About 40 effects in 17 seconds: a bell on the icon, typing, taps, a whoosh on every transition, a chime on “ready offline”, the same bell at the end so the ad loops. Quiet music under it all, a short reverb tail, loudness −14 LUFS. Use a sound pack you are allowed to use.

05 / checks

Check before you show anyone

  • Render a contact sheet of frames and open the doubtful ones up close: nothing overlaps, text is readable, nothing is cut off.
  • Show the storyboard before the video. It is faster to fix a still than a render.
  • Check every number and quote its source on screen.
  • Check the rights to the fonts, icons, music and sounds you use.
  • Label it an unofficial concept. Do not use a brand's name to suggest it paid for the ad.
  • Listen to the final sound yourself and watch the motion. A still frame cannot show either.
