Turn a private feeling into a route, a result, and a shared moment.

Strava is an app for recording activities and connecting with other athletes. This is an unofficial concept by Veronika. Strava did not commission it.

Materials first: runner photo and portrait photos for the activity feed, with permission to use them, plus a licensed reaction emoji graphic (or replace it with original artwork drawn in code). No voice. No licensed music track. The project synthesises its soundtrack and sound effects.

01 / brief

One wish becomes a run

The opening line is “I need to clear my head.” The phone turns that wish into an activity, then the activity becomes proof of progress and a reason for friends to connect.

How the reel works

The composition is a full-frame vertical ad. The orange circle grows into the app world. A countdown becomes the runner's route. The map and progress dot keep the eye moving through results, a personal-record medal, an activity post, kudos, and a group challenge. The final orange field resolves to the Strava mark.

Design system: Strava orange #FC4C02, near-black #17171A, warm paper #F1F0ED. Keep type sharp and motion smooth. The specific route, names, distances and ranking are story props from the project, not real athlete results.

Source timing

The source composition is set to 800 frames at 30 fps; the library preview contains 795 video frames (26.5 seconds). Use the preview as a visual reference and check the last 5 frames instead of assuming the ending matches exactly. The main ad advances at 0.75 source frames per output frame. Use the source scene anchors below as approximate output times, then check the final render.

Create a 1080 x 1920 Remotion concept reel. Begin with the typed thought “I need to clear my head.” Show a phone recording screen, an orange countdown, a route on a stylised city map, progress, split bars, a personal-record medal, a feed card with runner photography and portraits, kudos, and a group challenge. End on the line “If it’s not on Strava, it didn’t happen” and an orange Strava-style finish.

Use one visible visual idea to connect each beat: the orange dot grows, becomes a route marker, then becomes the final orange field. Use #FC4C02, #17171A and #F1F0ED. Keep words still and readable, with smooth easing and no shaking or text blur.

Ask me to supply or approve the runner photo and portraits before rendering. Treat all names, routes and activity numbers as fictional examples. Synthesise original sound effects and music in code. Do not assume a licensed track or a voice recording. Build a short draft, show key stills and ask me to inspect motion and sound locally before final output. Label the piece an unofficial concept by Veronika, not commissioned by Strava.

Give useful revision notes

“The orange dot jumps between the countdown and the route. Make its position continuous.”

“The feed photo is hard to read. Increase contrast without blurring the text.”

“Keep the PR, kudos and challenge beats long enough to understand.”

03 / checks

Check the story

  • Look at the first and last frames of each transition. The route and orange dot should move without unexplained jumps.
  • Check that the runner and portrait photos are supplied, approved, and credited where needed.
  • Read every number as an illustrative UI prop, never as a real user's result or a product promise.
  • Watch the draft yourself for rhythm. Listen to the soundtrack yourself; the guide does not claim to have heard it.
  • Confirm the unofficial-concept label is clear before sharing outside this project.
What the reference does not provide: reusable rights to the original Unsplash images or permission from the pictured people. Supply your own approved images.
